What Grover actually buys you

The pop-science version says a quantum computer tries every answer at once. If that were true, this would take one step. It doesn't. It takes about π/4 · √N rounds: a square-root speedup. Not O(1), not exponential. Quadratic.

From these cards to real RSA

  • Every card is two different primes multiplied together, the same shape as an RSA key: multiplying is easy, undoing it is hard.
  • The biggest card is 35, a 6-bit number, run on 8 qubits. A real RSA key is 2048 bits, about 617 digits.
  • Grover isn't the threat to RSA. Even with its square-root speedup, a 2048-bit key still needs on the order of 2512 rounds.
  • Shor's algorithm is, and the estimates keep falling: about 20 million noisy qubits for RSA-2048 (Gidney and Ekerå, 2019), then under a million (Gidney, 2025).
  • The hardware isn't there yet, which is why post-quantum cryptography is already rolling out ahead of it.

Squares like 9 = 3 × 3 work in the demo too, but RSA always uses two different primes, so the hand sticks to those.
